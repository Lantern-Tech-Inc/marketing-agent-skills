## Phase 11: Draft from evidence

Write the full draft only after the brief is complete.

### Opening

- Answer the query directly in 40–60 words.
- Name the intended audience and practical outcome.
- Avoid scene-setting, generic trend language, and unsupported urgency.

### Body

- Use one idea per paragraph and two to four sentences per paragraph.
- Make each H2 answer a real subquestion.
- Put comparisons in tables and processes in ordered steps.
- Explain acronyms on first use.
- Use exact entities, dates, and product names where relevant.
- Place evidence immediately beside the claim it supports.
- Add original screenshots, charts, examples, or templates where planned.
- Include limitations when evidence is directional or incomplete.

### AI-answer readiness

- Begin each major section with a concise standalone answer.
- Use explicit nouns instead of unclear pronouns.
- Keep definitions stable and checkable.
- Include source attribution in visible prose.
- Structure tables and lists so they remain understandable when extracted.
- Do not add special “AI text,” hidden summaries, or keyword blocks.

### Product integration

- Introduce the product only where it resolves the current section's problem.
- Demonstrate the relevant capability with a real screenshot or workflow.
- Use descriptive internal-link anchors.
- Do not interrupt every section with a CTA.
- Use one contextual mid-page CTA and one final CTA unless a commercial format requires otherwise.

### Conclusion

- State the decision or next action, not a generic recap.
- Connect the reader to the commercial hub or next logical spoke.
- Do not introduce new claims in the conclusion.

## Phase 12: Run five QA gates

### Intent QA

- The draft satisfies the dominant SERP intent.
- The content format matches the query.
- The promised answer appears early.
- No unrelated subtopic was added merely for length.

### Evidence QA

- Every statistic, quote, benchmark, price, and comparative claim has a source.
- Primary sources are preferred over pages repeating the claim.
- Product screenshots match current functionality.
- Dates, sample sizes, and limitations are visible.

### Search QA

- The primary keyword appears naturally in the title, H1, introduction, and one relevant H2.
- Secondary terms appear only where semantically appropriate.
- Metadata is unique and accurately previews the page.
- The canonical, language, and indexing directives are correct.
- The page does not cannibalize another URL.

### AI visibility QA

- Definitions and key answers are concise and extractable.
- Named entities are unambiguous.
- Claims are attributable and easy to verify.
- Original data or experience is present.
- FAQ answers add coverage instead of repeating body copy.

### Conversion QA

- The CTA matches the reader's stage and query.
- Product claims are demonstrated rather than asserted.
- Internal links connect the page to its commercial hub and sibling content.
- The next action is clear without overwhelming the article.
